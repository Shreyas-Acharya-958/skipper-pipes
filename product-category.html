Borewell Pipes

Product Overview

Skipper Borewell Pipes are built to protect your bore and deliver groundwater reliably for farms, homes and industry. Made from high-performance uPVC, the range brings together the three pipes every borewell needs: casing pipes that line and stabilise the bore, ribbed strainer pipes that let water in while holding back sand and gravel, and column pipes that carry the submersible pump and its discharge to the surface. Each pipe is corrosion-free, lightweight to handle and designed for leak-proof threaded joints, so installation is faster and the bore stays clean for years. Available in multiple diameters and pressure classes, Skipper Borewell Pipes can be matched to your bore depth, soil conditions and pump size.

A Complete Guide to Borewell Pipes

A borewell is only as dependable as the pipes that line it and lift water from it. Every bore uses three kinds of pipe working together. Casing pipes are lowered first to line the drilled hole, hold back loose soil and stop surface water from contaminating the aquifer. Ribbed strainer pipes are fitted at the water-bearing zone, where their slots admit groundwater while the ribs keep the filter pack in place. Column pipes then suspend the submersible pump and carry its discharge to the surface under pressure.

Getting the diameter, pressure class and joint type right for each of these pipes decides how long your borewell lasts and how much water it delivers. This guide walks you through each pipe, how to size it, and how to install it correctly.

Choosing the correct material and installation method also improves the overall performance and reliability of the borewell system.

Frequently Asked Questions

Borewell pipes are the pipes used to build and operate a borewell. They include casing pipes that line the drilled bore, ribbed strainer pipes that let groundwater in at the aquifer, and column pipes that hold the submersible pump and carry water to the surface.

Casing pipes line and protect the bore wall. Ribbed strainer pipes are slotted sections placed at the water-bearing layer to admit water while holding back sand and gravel. Column pipes sit inside the casing, suspend the submersible pump and carry the pumped water upward under pressure.

Casing diameter follows the drilled bore diameter, and column pipe size follows the pump outlet and required flow. Bore depth and pump weight decide the pressure class.

uPVC pipes do not rust or scale, are much lighter to handle and install, and keep water free from metallic contamination. GI pipes offer high mechanical strength but corrode over time, especially in hard or saline groundwater.

Safe installation depth depends on the pipe class and wall thickness, along with pump weight for column pipes.
